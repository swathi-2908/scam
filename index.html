CNNFebruary 4, 2024 — Hong Kong
A finance worker paid out $25 million after a video call with a deepfake "CFO"
Hong Kong police said the employee first grew suspicious of an email that claimed to be from the company's UK-based CFO. He assumed it was phishing.
That suspicion faded once he joined a video call with people who looked and sounded like colleagues he already knew. Over fifteen transfers, he sent HK$200 million (about $25.6 million) to five bank accounts. He only caught the fraud afterward, by checking with head office himself.
"Everyone you see is fake."

How to verify

How Chen could have verified.

Tap each one you'd actually use.

Call a known number

Use a number you already have on file — not the one provided in the message.

Use a second channel

Confirm through an independent, official system — not the same chat or call where the request came from.

Ask something only they'd know

A detail an impersonator — or a deepfake — wouldn't have access to.

Treat secrecy as a red flag

"Keep this confidential" is a tactic to stop you from checking, not a normal instruction.

Slow down on purpose

Urgency is designed to rush your judgment. A real request can survive a pause.
